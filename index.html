    WILL, WORDS & SYMBOLS

    Where the ideas come from.

    Aleister Crowley: begin with a clear will or intention. In Magick in Theory and Practice, he describes magick as causing change “in conformity with Will.” In Liber O, symbols and ritual objects reinforce a chosen idea; he calls the apparatus a system of mnemonics.

    Austin Osman Spare: the personal word-sigil method comes more directly from The Book of Pleasure (1913). He describes combining simplified letters into a form that is easy to visualize.

    A modern drawing recipe, with historical roots.

    Our word mode keeps each A–Z letter once, groups its shapes into lines, angles, curves and loops, then overlaps them in at most five strokes. This is our simplification of Spare’s idea, not a reconstruction of his or Crowley’s original sigils. Different intentions can share shapes.

    Historical mode preserves the chosen mark. Each library entry names its tradition and links its source. Glyphs are simplified conventional drawings; historic forms vary. Geometric art remains an optional decorative style.

    Use your symbol for reflection and remembering your goals. Historical practice does not establish that a symbol guarantees an outcome or that quantum physics makes manifestation work. Images are processed on your device. Download anything you want to keep.
